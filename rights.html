⚖️ Consumer Rights

Understanding your rights and what ethical online selling looks like.

Consumer Rights Awareness

As an online consumer, you have the right to expect fair and honest treatment from sellers. The following are general consumer rights that responsible sellers and platforms should uphold. Note that specific rights and remedies may vary depending on the platform, seller, and applicable laws.

Accurate Product Information

You have the right to receive honest and complete information about a product before purchasing — including its features, materials, dimensions, and any limitations. Sellers should not misrepresent what they are selling.

Fair and Transparent Prices

The price you see should reflect the actual cost of the item. All additional charges, such as shipping fees or handling fees, should be clearly disclosed before you confirm your purchase.

Safe and Reliable Transactions

You have the right to expect that your payment will be processed securely and that the item you paid for will be delivered as described. Platforms should provide mechanisms to protect buyers from fraud.

Clear Refund, Return, and Complaint Procedures

Sellers should clearly state their return and refund policies. You should know in advance what to do if an item arrives damaged, is not as described, or does not arrive at all.

Protection of Personal Information

Your personal data — including your name, address, contact number, and payment details — should be collected only for legitimate purposes and protected from unauthorized access or misuse.

Honest Advertising and Freedom from Misleading Claims

Advertisements should accurately represent the product. You should not be misled by exaggerated claims, fake before-and-after photos, or fabricated customer testimonials.

Ethical Advertising and Marketing

Ethical advertising builds trust between sellers and consumers. Here is a comparison of good and bad practices.

✔ Good Practices

  • Use real and accurate product photos taken by the seller.
  • State the price and all additional charges clearly and upfront.
  • Explain product features honestly, including any limitations.
  • Disclose sponsored promotions and paid endorsements.
  • Avoid misleading claims and fabricated reviews.
  • Provide clear and easy-to-understand return policies.

✘ Unethical Practices

  • Advertising fake discounts using inflated original prices.
  • Hiding shipping fees or other charges until checkout.
  • Making false or exaggerated claims about a product's benefits.
  • Purchasing fake reviews to mislead potential buyers.
  • Using misleading advertisements or high-pressure tactics.
  • Using stock photos that do not represent the actual product.

⚠ Why This Matters

Unethical advertising harms consumers financially and erodes trust in online commerce. As a consumer, recognizing these practices helps you make better purchasing decisions and hold sellers accountable.

Philippine Consumer Protection

Republic Act No. 7394 — The Consumer Act of the Philippines (1992)

The Consumer Act of the Philippines is the primary law protecting Filipino consumers. It establishes the rights of consumers and the responsibilities of businesses, and it covers areas such as product quality and safety, fair trade practices, honest labeling and advertising, and consumer complaint procedures.

The law is enforced by several government agencies, including the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), and the Department of Agriculture (DA), depending on the type of product or service involved.

While this law was enacted before the widespread use of e-commerce, its general principles — such as the right to accurate information, fair pricing, and protection from deceptive practices — continue to apply to online transactions in the Philippines.

Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) — Consumer Protection

The DTI handles consumer complaints related to trade and commerce, including online shopping disputes. Filipino consumers can file complaints through the DTI's official channels if they experience unfair trade practices, defective products, or deceptive advertising.
